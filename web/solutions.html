The thesis

Judgment, delivered as an oracle.

An oracle answers what is the price? — a fact a feed can read. A claims adjuster answers was the deliverable acceptable? — a subjective judgment a rigid rule can't make and a single off-chain bot can't be trusted to make. Verdikt is the second kind of oracle: a panel of Somnia validator LLM agents that each run the same inference and converge on a byte-identical verdict, then settle it programmatically.

Because the answer is one of a fixed set of labels — PAYEE / PAYER / SPLIT (with opt-in graded SPLIT25/50/75) and UNDECIDABLE when evidence is genuinely insufficient — any contract can consume the verdict the same way it consumes a price feed. That is the whole design: VerdiktCourt is a reusable arbitration primitive, not a single product.

Six deployed consumers · one verdict, many settlements

What the court arbitrates — and how each verdict pays out.

Explore the precedent

Each consumer opens a case with structured evidence, then maps the panel's label onto its own settlement. All six are real contracts in src/ and deployed on Shannon.

Two-party escrow

src/VerdiktEscrow.sol

A payer escrows funds for a payee against a delivery deadline. On dispute, the court rules who was right — and the losing party can appeal by staking (slashed if the original verdict holds).

PAYEE → release the deposit to the payee.
PAYER → refund the deposit to the payer.
SPLIT 25·50·75 → graded payout — the court can do justice, not just pick a winner.

Agent-to-agent

src/VerdiktAgentEscrow.sol

Machine-native escrow where both counterparties may be contracts or agents — the entire lifecycle is code-callable, with no EOA anywhere in the settlement path. Settles via pull payments so a non-receiving counterparty can't brick the court callback. Autonomous agents can't sue each other — now they can.

PAYEE / PAYER → credit the winning agent's pull-payment ledger.
live on Shannon → an A2A dispute that couldn't convene 5 settled at a resilient 3-validator panel → PAYER, with no human in the loop.

Insurance pool

src/VerdiktInsurance.sol

A collateralized claims pool: funders deposit and earn pro-rata shares, insureds buy policies and file claims with evidence. The panel arbitrates the subjective claim; either side can appeal by staking.

PAYEE → pay the insured full coverage from the pool.
SPLIT → pay the graded fraction of coverage.
PAYER → no payout — burden of proof is on the claimant.

Prediction market

src/examples/VerdiktPredictionMarket.sol

A binary market whose subjective outcome is resolved by the court instead of a trusted admin — "did the team ship by the deadline?", "was the announcement bullish?". The clearest case of judgment-as-an-oracle: the verdict is the resolution.

PAYEE → resolve YES; winners claim the pool pro-rata.
PAYER → resolve NO.
SPLIT / UNDECIDABLE → VOID — every staker is refunded.

DAO grant clawback

src/VerdiktGrantClawback.sol

A DAO escrows a grant for a grantee against a milestone deadline. If delivery is contested, either party disputes and the court decides whether the work earned the release.

PAYER → clawback the grant to the DAO funder.
PAYEE → release the milestone to the grantee.
SPLIT → graded share each; UNDECIDABLE claws back to the DAO.

Freelance milestone

src/VerdiktMilestone.sol

A client escrows payment for a freelancer against a due date. The dispute the court settles is the oldest one in freelancing: was the deliverable acceptable?

PAYEE → pay the freelancer.
PAYER → refund the client.
SPLIT → graded share each; UNDECIDABLE refunds the client.
One court, shared by all six

The network effect: shared precedent.

These aren't six forks of the same logic — they all call one VerdiktCourt. Every final ruling, whatever consumer raised it, is recorded as an immutable, citable precedent in the registry. An escrow dispute and a prediction-market resolution draw on, and add to, the same body of on-chain, AI-authored case law. The more protocols share the court, the deeper the precedent each one inherits.

Developer guide

Inherit VerdiktConsumerBase and implement one function — _settle(ref, verdict). The base handles the court wiring, the callback guard, opening a dispute (quote + fee + pull-refund), the caseId↔ref mapping, and decoding a graded, UNDECIDABLE-aware verdict into a payee basis-point share. The same pattern that powers all six consumers is under 50 lines for a new one.

01

Inherit the base

VerdiktConsumerBase(court_) wires your contract to the shared court.

02

Open a dispute

_openDispute(ref, evidence, payer) quotes the fee and opens the case.

03

Panel rules

The court convenes the validator panel and reaches byte-identical consensus.

04

Map the verdict

_payeeShareBps(ref, verdict) turns the label into a graded payout share.

05

Settle (pull)

Credit a pending[] ledger so a reverting recipient can't brick finalization.

FAQ

Questions.

How is this different from a price oracle?

A price oracle reports a fact a feed can read. Verdikt reports a verdict on a subjective question — "was the deliverable acceptable?", "is the claim valid?" — that no rigid rule can answer. The panel of validator agents converges on a byte-identical label, so a contract can consume that judgment the same way it consumes a feed.

Do all six consumers really share one court?

Yes. Escrow, agent-to-agent, insurance, prediction market, DAO grant clawback, and freelance milestone are six independent contracts in src/ that all call the same VerdiktCourt. Every final ruling lands in one shared precedent registry, so the case law compounds across domains.

What does each verdict actually mean for settlement?

The court returns one of a fixed set — PAYEE / PAYER / SPLIT (graded 25/50/75) / UNDECIDABLE. Each consumer maps it: escrow releases or refunds; the prediction market resolves YES/NO or VOID; insurance pays full, partial, or nothing; grants clawback or release; milestones pay the freelancer or refund the client.

Can I add a new protocol on top?

Yes — that's the point of a primitive. Inherit VerdiktConsumerBase and implement _settle; the base handles the court wiring, the callback guard, and decoding a graded verdict into a payout share. See the developer guide and the SDK.
